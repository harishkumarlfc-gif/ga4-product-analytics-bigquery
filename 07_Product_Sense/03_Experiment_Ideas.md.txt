# 🧪 Experiment Ideas

## Overview

Product Analytics identifies opportunities, but experiments determine whether proposed solutions create measurable business value.

Rather than implementing changes based on assumptions, Product teams validate ideas through controlled experiments.

Each experiment in this project follows the same structure:

```

Observation

↓

Hypothesis

↓

Experiment

↓

Primary Metric

↓

Guardrail Metrics

↓

Expected Business Impact

```

The experiments below are derived from insights generated throughout the Product Analytics dashboard.

---

# Experiment 1 — Simplify Checkout Experience

## Observation

- Checkout → Purchase Conversion: **38.2%**
- Checkout Abandonment: **61.8%**

Users reaching checkout have already demonstrated strong purchase intent, making this stage a high-impact optimization opportunity.

---

## Hypothesis

Reducing friction during checkout may increase the percentage of users completing their purchases.

---

## Experiment

A/B test a simplified checkout experience.

Possible improvements:

- Fewer checkout steps
- Simplified forms
- Improved mobile checkout
- Faster payment flow

---

## Success Metrics

### Primary Metric

- Checkout → Purchase Conversion Rate

### Guardrail Metrics

- Average Order Value (AOV)
- Revenue per Visitor

### Supporting Metrics

- Checkout Completion Time
- Payment Success Rate

---

## Expected Business Impact

Higher checkout conversion increases completed orders without requiring additional customer acquisition.

---

# Experiment 2 — Free Shipping Threshold

## Observation

Average Order Value:

**$68.91**

Increasing AOV allows revenue to grow without increasing traffic.

---

## Hypothesis

A free-shipping threshold slightly above the current AOV may encourage customers to add additional products before completing their purchase.

---

## Experiment

A/B test a free-shipping threshold approximately **1.25× the current AOV**.

Example:

Current AOV

$68.91

↓

Test Free Shipping Above

≈ $85

---

## Success Metrics

### Primary Metric

- Average Order Value (AOV)

### Guardrail Metrics

- Purchase Conversion Rate
- Contribution Margin (Production Environment)

### Supporting Metrics

- Products per Order
- Average Selling Price
- Orders Above Shipping Threshold

---

## Expected Business Impact

Increase basket value while maintaining healthy purchase conversion.

---

# Experiment 3 — Improve Repeat Purchases

## Observation

- One-time Buyers: **88.15%**
- Repeat Buyers: **11.85%**

Customer retention represents a significant opportunity for long-term revenue growth.

---

## Hypothesis

Providing a post-purchase incentive may encourage first-time buyers to return.

---

## Experiment

Send a personalized email containing a limited-time discount coupon **30 days after the customer's first purchase**.

---

## Success Metrics

### Primary Metric

- Repeat Purchase Rate

### Guardrail Metrics

- Average Order Value
- Discount Cost

### Supporting Metrics

- Email Open Rate
- Click-through Rate
- Revenue from Returning Customers

---

## Expected Business Impact

Increase customer retention while improving Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).

---

# Experiment 4 — Apparel Cross-Sell Recommendations

## Observation

Dashboard analysis showed:

- Apparel products generate the highest revenue.
- Accessories such as stickers, pens, and notebooks generate high sales volume.

Customers already purchase an average of **4.38 products per order**, indicating a willingness to buy multiple items in a single transaction.

---

## Hypothesis

Recommending inexpensive accessories alongside higher-value apparel may encourage impulse purchases and increase basket value.

---

## Experiment

Display personalized accessory recommendations during:

- Product Detail Page
- Shopping Cart
- Checkout

Examples:

- Hoodie → Sticker
- Hoodie → Pen
- Hoodie → Notebook Set

---

## Success Metrics

### Primary Metric

- Average Order Value (AOV)

### Guardrail Metrics

- Checkout Conversion Rate
- Revenue per Visitor

### Supporting Metrics

- Accessory Attachment Rate
- Apparel Revenue
- Products per Order

---

## Expected Business Impact

Increase basket value through low-friction impulse purchases without significantly affecting checkout completion.

---

# Key Learnings

This project highlights an important principle of Product Analytics:

> Data identifies opportunities.

> Experiments validate solutions.

Rather than assuming why users behave a certain way, Product Analysts formulate hypotheses, define measurable success metrics, and use controlled experiments to validate product decisions before large-scale implementation.
