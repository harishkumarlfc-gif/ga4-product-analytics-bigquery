# 📊 Case Study 01 — Prioritizing Revenue Growth

## Business Scenario

Assume you have recently joined the Google Merchandise Store as a Product Analyst.

The Product Manager has shared the latest business dashboard and asked a simple question:

> **"Revenue has been relatively flat. If you could improve only ONE area during the next month, what would you prioritize?"**

Using insights from the dashboard, this case study demonstrates how product decisions can be prioritized using data rather than assumptions.

---

# Step 1 — Understand the Business Goal

The primary business objective is to increase revenue.

Revenue can be increased by improving one or more of the following drivers:

- Increase completed orders
- Increase Average Order Value (AOV)
- Increase customer retention
- Increase website traffic

Since engineering resources are limited, only one initiative can be prioritized initially.

---

# Step 2 — Review Key Business Metrics

| Metric | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Revenue | $306,714 |
| Orders | 4,451 |
| Buyers | 3,713 |
| Average Order Value | $68.91 |
| Products per Order | 4.38 |
| Checkout Conversion | 38.2% |
| Checkout Abandonment | 61.8% |
| Repeat Buyers | 11.85% |

These metrics indicate multiple opportunities for improvement.

---

# Step 3 — Opportunity Assessment

## Opportunity 1 — Increase AOV

Increasing Average Order Value would improve revenue without increasing customer acquisition.

Possible initiatives include:

- Product bundles
- Cross-selling
- Free shipping thresholds
- Premium recommendations

---

## Opportunity 2 — Improve Customer Retention

Only **11.85%** of customers returned for another purchase.

Improving retention could increase Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) and reduce reliance on acquiring new customers.

---

## Opportunity 3 — Reduce Checkout Abandonment ✅

Checkout conversion is only **38.2%**, meaning approximately **61.8%** of users who begin checkout do not complete their purchase.

These users have already demonstrated strong purchase intent.

Improving conversion at this stage can directly increase completed orders without requiring additional marketing spend.

---

# Final Prioritization

## Priority 1

### Reduce Checkout Abandonment

### Why?

Users who reach checkout are the closest to completing a purchase.

Converting existing high-intent users is typically more cost-effective than acquiring additional traffic.

Potential initiatives include:

- Simplifying checkout
- Reducing form complexity
- Improving mobile checkout
- Optimizing payment flow

---

## Priority 2

### Improve Customer Retention

With **88.15%** of customers purchasing only once, retention represents a significant long-term growth opportunity.

Possible initiatives include:

- Personalized email campaigns
- Loyalty programs
- Post-purchase engagement
- Personalized recommendations

---

# Success Metrics

### Primary Metrics

- Checkout → Purchase Conversion Rate
- Repeat Purchase Rate

### Guardrail Metrics

- Average Order Value (AOV)
- Revenue per Visitor

### Supporting Metrics

- Checkout Completion Time
- Email Open Rate
- Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

---

# Key Takeaways

Rather than attempting to optimize every business metric simultaneously, Product Analytics helps prioritize opportunities based on expected business impact.

For this dataset, improving checkout conversion represents the highest-priority short-term opportunity because it targets users who have already demonstrated strong purchase intent.

Customer retention remains the second strategic priority for sustainable long-term growth.
