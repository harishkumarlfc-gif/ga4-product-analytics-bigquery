# 🔍 Root Cause Analysis (RCA)

## Overview

Product Analytics helps identify **where** users experience friction during their journey.

However, behavioral data alone does not always explain **why** a problem occurs.

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is a structured approach used to generate hypotheses based on observed user behavior before designing experiments to validate them.

Throughout this project, observations are clearly separated from hypotheses to avoid drawing unsupported conclusions.

---

# Root Cause Analysis Framework

Every business problem is analyzed using the following framework.

```

Observation

↓

Business Impact

↓

Possible Hypotheses

↓

Additional Data Required

↓

Experiment

↓

Success Metrics

```

This approach helps ensure that business decisions are based on evidence rather than assumptions.

---

# Case 1 — Checkout Abandonment

## Observation

The purchase funnel showed the following user journey:

| Funnel Stage | Users |
|--------------|------:|
| View Item | 61,252 |
| Add to Cart | 12,545 |
| Begin Checkout | 9,715 |
| Purchase | 3,713 |

Checkout → Purchase conversion:

**38.2%**

Checkout abandonment:

**61.8%**

---

## Business Impact

Users who reach the checkout stage have already demonstrated strong purchase intent.

Improving conversion at this stage has the potential to increase completed orders without requiring additional traffic acquisition.

---

## Possible Hypotheses

The available dataset does not identify the exact reason for abandonment.

Possible hypotheses include:

- Checkout UX friction
- Payment failures
- Limited payment methods
- Delivery-related concerns
- Unexpected shipping costs
- Technical issues during checkout

These hypotheses should be validated before implementing product changes.

---

## Additional Data Required

To validate the hypotheses above, additional product instrumentation may include:

- Payment success / failure events
- Checkout step completion events
- Error logs
- Shipping option selection
- Exit page analysis
- Device-specific checkout performance
- Session recordings or heatmaps

---

## Suggested Experiment

A/B test a simplified checkout experience.

Possible changes:

- Reduce checkout steps.
- Improve form usability.
- Simplify payment flow.
- Improve mobile checkout experience.

---

## Success Metrics

Primary Metric

- Checkout → Purchase Conversion Rate

Guardrail Metrics

- Average Order Value (AOV)
- Revenue per Visitor

Supporting Metrics

- Checkout Completion Time
- Payment Success Rate

# Case 2 — Low Repeat Purchase Rate

## Observation

Repeat purchase analysis showed:

- One-time Buyers: **88.15%**
- Repeat Buyers: **11.85%**

Cohort analysis also indicated low customer retention after the first purchase.

---

## Business Impact

Acquiring new customers is typically more expensive than retaining existing ones.

Improving repeat purchases increases Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) while reducing long-term dependence on customer acquisition.

---

## Possible Hypotheses

Potential reasons customers may not return include:

- Better competing alternatives
- Lack of purchase incentives
- Limited customer engagement
- Product satisfaction
- Delivery experience
- Users forgetting the brand over time

The current dataset does not provide sufficient evidence to determine the primary cause.

---

## Additional Data Required

Examples of useful data include:

- Customer feedback
- Product reviews
- NPS surveys
- Email campaign engagement
- Loyalty program participation
- Customer support interactions

---

## Suggested Experiment

A/B test a personalized email campaign that sends a limited-time discount to first-time buyers 30 days after purchase.

---

## Success Metrics

Primary Metric

- Repeat Purchase Rate

Guardrail Metrics

- Average Order Value (AOV)
- Discount Redemption Cost

Supporting Metrics

- Email Open Rate
- Click-through Rate
- Revenue from Returning Customers

---

# Key Takeaways

This analysis demonstrates an important principle in Product Analytics:

**Behavioral data identifies where problems occur.**

**Experiments identify why they occur.**

Separating observations from hypotheses helps teams avoid incorrect assumptions and prioritize data-driven product decisions.
